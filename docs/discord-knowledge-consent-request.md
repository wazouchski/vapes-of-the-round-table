# Asking the Discord community first

Status: **draft for the board.** Nothing here has been posted. No Discord
content has been read, stored, or used, and none will be until the community
has answered.

The board's instruction was: hold, the community needs to be asked before
anything. This is the ask, written so a human can post it.

## Why ask rather than assume

The server is owned by the board and maintained by a member, so we likely have
the *authority* to read it. That is not the same as having the community's
agreement. People posted their setups, their failures, and their opinions to a
chat room, not to a public reference site that ranks in search results. Taking
that material without asking would be the fastest way to lose the trust the
wiki is trying to earn — and trust is the whole product.

There is also a practical reason. Consent given once, publicly, is durable.
Content taken quietly has to be defended every time someone notices.

## What we would actually want

Worth being specific, because a vague ask gets a vague answer:

- **Recurring knowledge, not conversations.** "Three owners found the screen
  loosens after about six months" — not the thread it came from.
- **Attribution by choice.** Credited by Discord handle, credited under another
  name, or uncredited. Per person, and changeable later.
- **A way out.** Anyone can have their contribution removed, with no argument
  and no explanation required.
- **Never the private parts.** No DMs, no mod channels, no vendor channels, no
  anything behind a role gate.

## Draft post

> **Something we'd like to ask you about before we do it**
>
> A lot of genuinely useful knowledge lives in this server — the fix for the
> thing that always breaks, which device actually holds up, the trick nobody
> writes in a manual. Most of it gets said once and then scrolls away.
>
> We'd like to start carrying some of that over to theroundtable.wiki, so it
> survives and so people outside the server can find it. But we're not going to
> do that without asking you first.
>
> To be clear about what we mean:
>
> - We'd take **recurring, useful knowledge** — not conversations, not screenshots,
>   not drama. Something like "several owners report the screen loosens after
>   about six months," not a copy of the thread.
> - We'd mark it as **community experience**, not as fact. The wiki keeps those
>   separate on purpose.
> - **You choose the credit.** Your handle, another name, or nothing at all.
> - **You can pull it back** at any time, and we'll remove it. You don't have to
>   give a reason.
> - **Nothing private.** No DMs, no mod channels, no role-gated channels.
>
> If you'd rather none of your posts were used, say so — here or in DM — and
> we'll respect it without asking why.
>
> Tell us what you think. If the room isn't comfortable with this, we won't do
> it.

## What we need back from the board

1. Approve or edit the post above.
2. Decide **who posts it.** It should come from a human the server knows, not
   from an agent and not from a fresh account.
3. Decide **how long to leave it open**, and what counts as agreement. A
   suggestion, not a decision: two weeks, and the absence of sustained objection
   plus an opt-out list we honour permanently.
4. Decide **where the opt-out list lives** so it cannot be lost when the
   maintainer changes.

## Until then

Objective 3 stays at zero, by choice rather than by accident. No Discord
integration will be requested and no Discord content will be read. If the
community says yes, the next blocker is technical — read access to the server —
and that is worth requesting only after the answer is yes.
