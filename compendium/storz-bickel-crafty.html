# Storz & Bickel Crafty

Overview

The Storz & Bickel Crafty is a compact portable dry herb vaporizer manufactured by the German company Storz & Bickel, best known as the creators of the Volcano desktop vaporizer. Released in late 2014 alongside its larger sibling the Mighty, the Crafty employs a hybrid convection/conduction heating system and is powered by a single internal lithium-ion battery. The device was later succeeded by the Crafty+ (Crafty Plus), which featured incremental improvements.

The Crafty was designed as the more pocket-friendly alternative to the Mighty, sharing the same heating element and oven design but housed in a smaller form factor with a single battery instead of two. Temperature control is managed primarily through a companion smartphone app (Bluetooth-connected), with the device's single physical button providing power on/off and a single boost temperature function.

The community widely regarded the Crafty as one of the best-performing portable vaporizers available during its era, frequently describing it as the "king of portable cloud generators." However, this praise was consistently tempered by significant concerns about battery life and long-term reliability.

Community Reception

Praise

The community consistently praised the Crafty for its vapor quality, noting it produced dense, flavorful clouds that rivaled or exceeded many desktop vaporizers. One community member described it as the "hardest hitting flower portable I have ever owned." The free-flowing draw resistance was widely appreciated, making it accessible even to users with respiratory limitations — it was specifically recommended for a patient with ALS due to its ease of draw. Community members noted the device was particularly effective at producing thick vapor even from short draws, a quality that set it apart from many competing portables.

The heating system — shared with the Mighty — was regarded as best-in-class for portable vaporizers. Multiple community members endorsed Storz & Bickel as "the king of the portable vaporizer market." The Crafty's compact size relative to its performance was seen as a significant advantage over the Mighty for users prioritizing true portability and pocket-carry.

Criticisms

The Crafty's battery life was its most consistently criticized feature. Community members noted the single battery provided very limited session count per charge, with one experienced member remarking that the short battery life was a significant reason they "switched back to flame vapes." Unlike the Mighty, the Crafty cannot be used while charging (pass-through charging), which compounded the battery limitation.

Reliability was the other major area of concern. The community discussed at length the frequency of malfunctions, with one member going so far as to say they "wouldn't call the Crafty reliable" and suggesting that "most" Crafty units eventually experienced issues. The area where the cooling unit connects to the main body was identified as a structural weak point prone to cracking or breaking with repeated use. The reliance on a smartphone app for full temperature control was seen as both a convenience and a vulnerability.

The device's efficiency relative to material consumption was debated. While it extracted thoroughly, one Crafty owner noted it was "a little too inefficient (burns through my stash too quick) to be used as a daily driver," suggesting the chamber size encouraged using more material than necessary for solo use.

Tips & Techniques

Loading

  • Grind fine to medium for best extraction. The hybrid heating system benefits from increased surface area.
  • A full, firm pack produces the best clouds, but overpacking restricts airflow.
  • For efficiency, the community suggested using the liquid pad (included) on top of smaller loads to keep material in contact with heating surfaces when using less than a full chamber.

Drawing Technique

  • The Crafty's low draw resistance allows for natural, relaxed inhales — slow, steady sipping was preferred for flavor, while slightly faster draws produced thicker clouds.
  • One user reported that deeply inhaling continuously "until the light turns red" and then resuming as soon as it turned green again extracted a bowl very quickly but caused throat irritation. A more moderate approach — taking a few draws, pausing, then continuing — was generally recommended.
  • Taking a hit or two after powering off while the unit is still within vaping temperature range was noted as a way to reduce waste, as residual heat continues to produce vapor briefly.

Session Management

  • As a session vaporizer, the Crafty continues to heat material between draws. The community acknowledged that some vapor is inevitably lost during pauses between hits. Users concerned about efficiency were advised to consider on-demand devices for single-hit use patterns.
  • Powering off between hits and taking advantage of residual heat was recommended as a partial mitigation strategy for vapor waste.

Battery Conservation

  • Given the limited battery life, the community recommended keeping a USB power source handy for recharging between sessions.
  • Avoiding extended sessions at boost temperatures helped preserve battery life.

Maintenance & Cleaning

Cooling Unit

The cooling unit is the primary maintenance focus. It collects resin and residue from vapor condensation and should be cleaned regularly — approximately every 1–2 weeks with daily use.

Disassemble the cooling unit (it separates into several components).

Soak all plastic and metal parts in isopropyl alcohol (90%+ recommended) for 15–30 minutes.

Rinse thoroughly with warm water and allow to dry completely before reassembly.

Replace screens as needed when they become clogged beyond effective cleaning.

Oven Chamber

  • Use the included brush to sweep out AVB (already vaped bud) after each session while the unit is still slightly warm.
  • Periodically swab the oven with a cotton swab dipped in isopropyl alcohol.

General Body

  • Wipe down the exterior with a damp cloth. Avoid submerging the unit.
  • The area around the cooling unit connection point should be kept free of debris to prevent stress on the housing.

Common Issues & Fixes

Short Battery Life / Battery Degradation

Problem: The single-cell battery degrades over time, eventually failing to hold a charge sufficient for even a single session. This was the most commonly reported long-term issue.

Solutions:

  • Custom battery packs: A community member known for assembling replacement battery packs offered pre-built drop-in replacements. These custom packs were assembled using quality cells and a spot welder, specifically sized for the Crafty.
  • DIY battery replacement: Some users attempted replacing the cell with a Sony VTC6 or similar 18650 cell. However, simply swapping cells sometimes caused issues — one user reported their device showed a full charge but blinked 7 times (empty battery indicator) and would not power on after a VTC6 swap. The custom-assembled packs with proper configuration were more reliable.
  • Storz & Bickel service: S&B offered battery replacement services under warranty. However, community members reported that out-of-warranty units were often not eligible for battery-only replacement, with S&B instead offering a discounted replacement unit (e.g., €248 plus the old device for a Mighty).

Housing Crack at Cooling Unit Connection

Problem: The plastic housing where the cooling unit attaches was identified as a structural weak point. Repeated attachment/removal of the cooling unit — especially if rotated too far — could cause cracks.

Fix:

  • Handle the cooling unit gently; do not over-rotate.
  • S&B honored warranty replacements for this issue during the warranty period.
  • One community member speculated that attaching the cooling unit backwards may have contributed to premature fatigue of this area.

Battery Temperature Sensor Failure

Problem: The battery temperature sensor (a 10k thermistor with a JST connector) uses extremely fine gauge wire that is prone to breaking, especially during battery replacement procedures. Symptoms include blue/red flashing lights and app messages reading "battery too low, warm up vaporizer" even when the device is hot.

Fix:

  • One community member successfully repaired a broken sensor by using solid breadboard jumper wire inserted under the original crimp in the connector, then securing everything with epoxy as a permanent fix.
  • Replacement thermistors and JST crimp pins can be sourced from electronics suppliers such as DigiKey, but the extreme small scale of the components makes DIY crimping very difficult without professional tools. Community members reported a very low success rate (approximately 1 in 10 attempts) with consumer-grade crimp tools.
  • Salvaging sensors from dead Mighty or Crafty units was suggested as the most practical source for replacements.

Error Codes

Err 04: Reported on first use by at least one community member. Resolved by holding the power button to switch off, waiting a few minutes, and restarting. This appeared to be an intermittent startup issue rather than a persistent hardware failure.

Firmware Suggestions

Community members expressed a desire for additional boost levels, suggesting a firmware update could implement multiple boost temperatures activated by sequential button presses (e.g., 2nd press for first boost, 3rd press for second boost, etc.). It is unclear whether Storz & Bickel implemented this in later firmware updates or the Crafty+.

Accessories & Modifications

Water Pipe Adapters (WPA)

Although the Crafty does not natively connect to glass water pieces as easily as some competitors, aftermarket WPA solutions were available. The "Easy Flow Water Tool Adapter" designed for the Crafty and Mighty was noted in the community as a solution that replaced the standard mouthpiece and could also function as a glass mouthpiece for dry use.

Community members reported that using the Crafty with a bubbler significantly improved comfort, particularly at higher temperatures, by cooling and humidifying the vapor. However, at least one user noted that the increased airflow through water caused them to draw more aggressively, which introduced a different set of issues (gum and throat irritation from volume of hot air).

Dosing Capsules

Storz & Bickel's dosing capsules were compatible with the Crafty, allowing pre-loaded sessions and reduced oven cleaning. These were popular among community members who valued convenience and cleanliness.

Custom Battery Packs

A community member offered custom-assembled battery packs as drop-in replacements for the internal battery. These were built with quality cells and proper spot-welded connections, priced at approximately $40–45 USD for a single Crafty pack (or $70 USD for two packs for the Mighty). This service was praised by the community as offering "better customer service than Storz & Bickel for veterans out of warranty."

A suggestion was raised about developing a battery sled system that would allow tool-free battery swaps, but this was deemed impractical given the limited internal space of Storz & Bickel devices.

Whip Attachment

Using a whip with the Crafty was suggested as another vapor cooling method for users experiencing throat irritation, though this was less commonly discussed than water filtration.

Comparisons

Crafty vs. Mighty

The most frequently discussed comparison in the community. Both devices share the same heating element and oven design, producing essentially identical vapor quality.

The community generally recommended the Mighty for home and heavy use, particularly for its pass-through charging capability and longer battery life. The Crafty was recommended when true portability and pocket-size were priorities. As one member succinctly put it, the Crafty is the Mighty's "baby brother" that "meets most of your requirements at a lower price."

Crafty vs. Arizer Air / Solo

The Arizer devices were noted for superior efficiency with small loads and replaceable batteries, but the Crafty was consistently described as producing denser, heavier-hitting vapor with less draw resistance. The Arizer stems were noted to hold less material. One user observed that while the Air "gets you more out of your bud" in quality comparison, session vapes like the Crafty delivered heavier effects faster. The Arizer line was sometimes preferred for its simplicity, lower price, glass vapor path, and user-replaceable batteries.

Crafty vs. Arizer ArGo

The ArGo was described as "a sipper" while the Mighty/Crafty family was characterized as "rippers." The ArGo offered superior portability, replaceable batteries, and easier cleaning, while the Crafty/Mighty excelled in raw vapor production and group session suitability.

Crafty vs. Boundless CFX

When a user transitioning from combustion asked about choosing between the Arizer Air, Crafty, and Boundless CFX, community responses generally acknowledged the Crafty's superior vapor quality while questioning whether the price premium was justified for all users. The DynaVap M was also suggested as a better entry point for users transitioning from combustion due to its similar ritual and feel.

Crafty vs. DaVinci Ascent

The Ascent offered a larger chamber, but the community considered the Crafty to deliver "bigger heavier hits." The Crafty was generally seen as the superior performer, while the Ascent was noted for its glass-lined chamber and all-glass vapor path.

Crafty vs. On-Demand Vaporizers (MFLB, Firefly, Grasshopper)

For users who only took a few hits at a time, the community frequently pointed out that session vaporizers like the Crafty inevitably waste some material between draws. On-demand devices like the MFLB, Firefly, and Grasshopper were recommended as more efficient alternatives for single-hit or intermittent use patterns. However, the Crafty was acknowledged as superior in total vapor quality and consistency when used in full sessions.

This article is based on community discussions spanning from late 2014 through 2022, primarily focused on the original Crafty model. Some information may also apply to the Crafty+ revision. As with all community-sourced knowledge, individual experiences varied, and readers are encouraged to consult multiple sources when making purchasing decisions.
