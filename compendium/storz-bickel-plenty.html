# Storz & Bickel Plenty

Overview

The Storz & Bickel Plenty is a corded, handheld desktop vaporizer manufactured by the German company Storz & Bickel, known primarily for the Volcano. The Plenty occupies a unique niche in the vaporizer market — it is AC-powered (plugged into the wall) but designed to be held in the hand rather than placed on a tabletop, giving it a form factor sometimes described as resembling a power tool or large heat gun. It uses a hybrid convection/conduction heating system and delivers vapor through a distinctive stainless steel cooling coil that terminates in a mouthpiece.

The Plenty was generally regarded within the community as a capable and powerful vaporizer that excelled in group settings due to its large herb chamber and robust vapor production. However, its high material consumption and unconventional design made it polarizing. As one community member put it, the Plenty is "a different animal than just about any other vape on the market" and functions "rather differently" from most units — a sentiment echoed repeatedly across years of discussion. Some devoted users considered it the best vaporizer available, while others found it inefficient for solo use.

Community Reception

The community was notably divided on the Plenty, though nearly all users acknowledged its raw vapor production capability. Discussion threads spanning from 2013 through 2023 reveal a consistent set of opinions:

Strengths widely acknowledged:

  • Exceptional flavor and potency. Users frequently praised the Plenty's vapor quality, particularly with dry herb and bubble hash sandwiches. One user described the "amazing flavor and potency" as standout features.
  • Massive cloud production. The Plenty was regularly cited as one of the best vaporizers for producing visible, dense, "smoke-like clouds" — a quality particularly valued by users transitioning from combustion.
  • Group vaporizer par excellence. The large chamber and robust output made the Plenty a natural choice for sessions with multiple people. Many users described it as their go-to "party vape."
  • Durability and reliability. The Plenty was described as a "solid workhorse" that could operate for extended periods without issue. Users reported years of consistent performance.
  • Accessibility. The analog temperature dial was noted as especially suitable for users who could not rely on digital displays, with one discussion recommending it specifically for blind users due to the tactile temperature wheel.
  • Desktop power in a handheld form. Community members appreciated the ability to pick up and pass around a mains-powered unit, contrasting it favorably with stationary desktops connected to whips.

Weaknesses frequently discussed:

  • High herb consumption. This was the most consistently raised criticism across all threads. The large chamber encouraged heavy loading, and even with the liquid pad used as a spacer, consumption was notably higher than most competing vaporizers. One experienced user noted that loading 0.3–0.4g worked "amazingly well," but the chamber capacity tempted users into loading much more.
  • Not ideal for solo sessions. Multiple users described purchasing the Plenty for group use and then seeking a more efficient vaporizer for personal sessions. The community frequently recommended more conservative alternatives for solo users.
  • Imprecise temperature control. The analog dial (numbered 1–7) received criticism for being "really quite shitty" in the words of one user, lacking the precision of digital controls found on competing products.
  • Not designed for water filtration. Unlike many desktop vaporizers, the Plenty does not feature a native glass-on-glass joint, making connection to water pipes and bubblers awkward and requiring DIY solutions.
  • Slow extraction style. The Plenty was described by some as best suited for "longer session 'medicinal' type vaping" rather than quick on-demand hits. One community member cautioned that it was "not suited to the purpose of the vast majority of vapers" if "time and speed are an important factor."
  • Size and cord dependency. Despite being handheld, the Plenty's power cord and bulk made it impractical for true portability. One user described being "tired of lugging it and its cord around town."

Despite these criticisms, the Plenty earned a devoted following. A passionate community member stated: "to Me personally, and perhaps quite a few others, it's the Best Vape on the market! In a Class of its own. And I Doubt that there is any way to improve it, as it was designed to operate."

Tips & Techniques

Loading

  • Optimal solo load: Community experience suggested 0.3–0.4g as a sweet spot for solo use, delivering great flavor, dense hits, and big clouds without excessive waste.
  • Use the liquid pad as a spacer: For smaller loads, place the included liquid pad on top of the herb in the chamber to reduce the effective chamber volume. This was the most commonly recommended technique for improving efficiency, though some users noted it still did not make the Plenty as conservative as smaller-chambered vaporizers.
  • Hash sandwiches: Several users reported excellent results with bubble hash layered between herb ("sandwich" method) in the Plenty's chamber, praising the resulting flavor and potency.
  • Grind consistency: A medium grind was generally preferred. The large chamber and airflow characteristics of the Plenty are forgiving of various grind sizes, but a consistent medium grind promotes even extraction.

Draw Technique

  • Slow, steady draws were preferred for the Plenty. The stainless steel cooling coil is designed to cool vapor over its length, and a measured draw rate allows the heating element to maintain temperature.
  • Multiple small sips rather than one massive rip were recommended, particularly for users experiencing throat irritation. This technique was emphasized in discussions involving singers and vocalists concerned about throat health.

Session Management

  • The Plenty was noted to perform best as a session vaporizer: turn it on, let it heat up, and draw from it over an extended period (30–45 minutes was a commonly described session length).
  • One experienced user strongly advised that the Plenty "really does perform best when used with the stock coil whip," cautioning against modifications that bypass the cooling coil.

Transitioning from Combustion

  • The Plenty was frequently recommended for users coming from joints, blunts, and bongs due to its substantial cloud production and familiar hand-to-mouth ritual. One user credited the Plenty with helping them "give up combustion" entirely.

Temperature Guide

The Plenty uses an analog dial numbered 1 through 7. The maximum temperature is approximately 408°F / 209°C per the manufacturer's specifications (with up to ±3% variation).

Dial SettingApproximate TemperatureUse Case
3–4~340–360°F / ~171–182°CFlavor-focused sessions; lighter effects; best for terpene preservation
5–6~370–395°F / ~188–202°CBalanced flavor and vapor density; moderate effects
7 (Maximum)~400–408°F / ~204–209°CMaximum extraction; densest clouds; strongest effects

Community sweet spot: Many users reported running the Plenty at the maximum setting (7) for best results, particularly with average-quality herb. One experienced user stated: "I always turn it up to 7 (max) to achieve this. Generally I find 7 to be the best setting but this may vary from user to user."

For throat sensitivity: Users experiencing harshness or vocal strain were advised to reduce temperature significantly and take smaller hits. Running below the maximum was described as producing "much less irritating" vapor.

Maintenance & Cleaning

The community did not document elaborate cleaning rituals for the Plenty as extensively as for some other vaporizers, but key maintenance points emerged:

  • Cooling coil: The stainless steel cooling coil accumulates residue over time and should be periodically cleaned. Soaking in isopropyl alcohol (90%+) followed by thorough rinsing and drying was the standard approach.
  • Chamber screen: Keep the basket screen in the filling chamber clean for optimal airflow and flavor. Brushing after each session and periodic ISO soaks were recommended.
  • Mouthpiece: The plastic mouthpiece should be cleaned regularly to prevent buildup that affects flavor.
  • General: Due to the primarily metal and plastic construction (no glass in the vapor path), the Plenty was considered relatively low-maintenance compared to glass-heavy setups. The robust construction meant fewer fragile parts to worry about.

Common Issues & Fixes

Throat Irritation / Dry Throat

This was the most commonly reported issue, discussed across multiple threads. The Plenty was noted to produce dry, potentially harsh vapor, especially at higher temperatures.

Solutions:

  • Lower the temperature dial
  • Take smaller, more frequent sips rather than large draws
  • Stay well-hydrated — drink plenty of water before, during, and after sessions
  • Consider adding water filtration via a DIY bubbler attachment (see Accessories section)

High Herb Consumption

Many users were surprised by how much material the Plenty consumed compared to their previous vaporizers.

Solutions:

  • Use the liquid pad as a chamber spacer to reduce effective bowl size
  • Load 0.3–0.4g rather than filling the entire chamber
  • Consider the Plenty primarily for group sessions and use a more efficient vaporizer for solo use

Not Truly Portable

Some users initially believed the Plenty could serve as a portable vaporizer due to its handheld form factor, only to discover its dependence on mains power.

Clarification: The Plenty is strictly a corded device. While it can be picked up and passed around a room, it requires a wall outlet at all times. Users seeking true portability were universally directed toward battery-powered alternatives.

Accessories & Modifications

Water Pipe Adapters (WPA)

The Plenty does not include a native glass adapter (14mm or 18mm), and this was a commonly discussed limitation. Community members explored several solutions:

  • DIY bubbler attachment: One technique involved removing the mouthpiece, inverting the Plenty, and inserting the open end of the cooling coil into a bubbler's bowl joint. While functional, this was acknowledged as awkward and imprecise. Users described needing to hold the assembly together manually.
  • Aftermarket adapters: Community members inquired about and occasionally sourced third-party adapters to connect the Plenty's output to standard glass joints, though no single widely adopted commercial solution emerged during the period of active discussion.
  • Hydratube compatibility: Some users reported that VapeXhale hydratubes could be fitted to the Plenty, though video evidence of this producing results comparable to a dedicated unit like the EVO was not widely available.

The community consensus was that the Plenty was "not ideal for pairing with water" and that users who primarily wanted water-filtered vapor would be better served by a vaporizer with native glass-on-glass connections.

Liquid Pad

The included Storz & Bickel liquid pad served double duty: it was designed for use with concentrates but became an essential accessory for solo users who wanted to reduce the effective chamber size. Placing it on top of a smaller load of herb was the most frequently recommended efficiency modification.

Whip Modifications

While the Plenty comes with its own stainless steel cooling coil (sometimes described as "whip-like"), some users experimented with attaching actual whip tubing to extend the vapor path or redirect it to other delivery systems. Community members noted that the stock coil configuration generally performed best.

Comparisons

Plenty vs. Mighty

The Mighty was the most frequently suggested alternative to the Plenty, especially for solo users. One community member told a prospective buyer to "look into the Mighty rather than the Plenty... it hits just as hard in a small little package." The community generally agreed that the Mighty offered comparable vapor quality in a smaller, more efficient, battery-powered format. The Plenty's advantages over the Mighty were limited to larger chamber capacity for groups and unlimited session length due to mains power. Users noted the Mighty could also be used while plugged in (pass-through charging), partially negating the Plenty's power advantage.

Plenty vs. Volcano

As stablemates from the same manufacturer, these two were naturally compared. The Volcano used a bag delivery system while the Plenty used direct-draw with a cooling coil. The Volcano was considered superior for group sessions by some community members who valued the convenience of passing around filled bags versus a corded handheld device. The Plenty was seen as more immediate and hands-on, with a different vapor delivery experience. Both were considered high-consumption devices.

Plenty vs. Da Buddha (DBV) / Silver Surfer (SSV)

The 7th Floor vaporizers (DBV and SSV) were frequently recommended as alternatives that offered comparable or superior value. One experienced community member stated they "would easily buy [the DBV] instead of the Plenty, even if the Plenty were the same price" and described the DBV as "the best buy there is in an AC powered big hitting whip vape." The SSV was described as hitting "like a train" and "like an EQ on steroids." Both 7th Floor units featured standard glass connections for easy water pipe integration — a significant advantage over the Plenty for users wanting water filtration.

Plenty vs. Life Saber (LSV)

The LSV was a direct competitor in the "corded handheld" category. Community opinion generally favored the LSV for its all-glass vapor path, better compatibility with bubblers and water pipes, and strong vapor production. The LSV was typically available at a significantly lower price point. One user suggested the LSV "over the Plenty," noting both were "just too big" for handheld use. However, the Plenty's larger chamber gave it an edge for extended group sessions.

Plenty vs. Log Vapes (E-Nano, Underdog, WoodScents)

Log vapes were frequently recommended as superior alternatives for solo users, offering dramatically better efficiency with very small loads (0.04–0.1g). The trade-off was smaller bowl capacity and longer heat-up times. One user stated the E-Nano was "approx $150 cheaper and Imo is a vastly superior vaporizer as well as being much more efficient" compared to the Plenty. The Plenty retained advantages in chamber capacity and group session capability.

Plenty vs. Extreme Q

Both were considered in purchase decisions for desktop use. The Extreme Q offered both whip and bag delivery modes at a lower price point, making it more versatile. However, the Plenty was generally considered to produce denser vapor. The Extreme Q's dual functionality was often cited as advantageous for group use, where bags could be passed around more safely than a corded device.
